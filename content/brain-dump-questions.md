# Brain-dump questions (optional)

The site copy is already written (`content/site-copy.md`), and the runbook's truth check covers the
lines only you can confirm. Use these questions only if you have a spare 10–20 minutes and want more of
the site in your own words. Anything you dictate here replaces my wording.

**How to do it**

1. Open a blank note (Apple Notes or a Google Doc). Start a screen recording with your mic on. This is
   footage for the video.
2. Hold Flow's hotkey and answer each question out loud, the way you'd tell a friend. Don't edit as
   you go, and don't read from anything.
3. About a minute per question. Skip anything you can't answer; a skipped question is better than an
   invented answer.
4. Paste the whole note, unedited, into `content/raw/2026-10-30-braindump.md`. Claude tightens it in
   your words; you approve the result.

Watch for one thing as you go. When Flow gets a word wrong (Skipp, Drishti, Kattankulathur…), note it
in `build-log/voice-log.md`. That's part of the "where voice broke" story.

## 1. Opening (2 questions)

1. In one breath: what do you build, and who do you build it for?
2. Why a growth role, and why now? What made you care about who *finds* a product, not just who builds it?

## 2. Skipp

3. What problem did you see on campus before you wrote a line of code?
4. Why were you the person to fix it?
5. How did the first students find out about Skipp? Name the actual moments: a group, a friend, a post.
6. What did you build or change because of something a student said or did?
7. What surprised you about how students actually use it? (Your numbers: 7–14 people a day, 84% on
   phones, over 92% coming back directly.)
8. What didn't work? (For example: no way to grow beyond word of mouth, the portal blocking you, push
   notifications you chose not to build.)
9. If you started Skipp again tomorrow, what would you do differently to reach more students?

## 3. Drishti

10. Who is Drishti for, and what is broken for them today?
11. What did building for 11 Indian languages teach you about how people in India talk to machines?
12. Tell the story of the 40/40 replay vs the 32/40 live run. Why do you publish the lower number?
13. Has a blind or low-vision person tried it yet? If not, what's stopping you, and what's the plan?

## 4. Recoup

14. Explain Recoup to a friend who has never heard of a failed payment.
15. Tell the story of the version that credited itself with a recovery it hadn't caused. How did you
    notice, and what did you change?
16. Your ML model lost to a 20-line rule. How did that feel, and what did you take from it?

## 5. What doesn't fit on a resume (setbacks)

17. Pick two or three real setbacks: a rejection, a hackathon you didn't win, a project that flopped.
    For each one: what happened, what you decided afterwards, and what changed because of it.
18. Can each company or event be named publicly? Say yes or no for each.

## 6. How you decide

For each, one real moment from your own work:

19. "Count what you caused, not what happened."
20. "Publish the real number, not the nice one."
21. "Use the simplest thing that works, then measure it."

## 7. Voice and India

22. How do the people around you use voice today: voice notes, Google voice typing, nothing? Think
    about your parents, friends, juniors.
23. When do you speak Tanglish, and when do you switch to English? Where would voice typing break for
    you?
24. If you had one month at Wispr Flow India, what's the first thing you'd test, and why?

## 8. Logistics

25. Your exact start date, and what you've confirmed with your department (OD, NOC, January).
26. Say it plainly: "I'll relocate to Bangalore for the full internship."

## 9. Loose ends

27. Vision2Web: was it Allotiq or Echo, and was it 2nd place or the win?
28. Pagdandi: is there anything built or written that exists today? If not, we leave it out.
29. OneClick and Samsung PRISM: what happened in the demo round?

## 10. Last one

30. If they remember one sentence about you, what should it be?
