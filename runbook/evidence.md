# Part 4 · The four small tests

Each one takes 15 minutes or less. You do the speaking and recording; I do the scoring and the
write-up. The predictions were written down before any test (`evidence/hypotheses.md`), so the results
can't be bent to fit.

## E-0 · Voice baseline (Friday, 10 minutes)

1. **Start the screen recording:** Cmd + Shift + 5 → Options → Microphone: MacBook microphone → Record
   Entire Screen.
2. **Typing.** Open Apple Notes. Start a stopwatch, type this paragraph exactly, then stop the
   stopwatch.
3. **Speaking.** New note. Start the stopwatch, hold your Flow key and say the same paragraph at a
   normal pace, then let go and stop the stopwatch.

> I built Skipp for SRM students because the portal never tells you how many classes you can still
> miss. Drishti is a voice agent for blind users in eleven Indian languages, built on Sarvam. Recoup
> recovers failed payments for Razorpay merchants. This week I have exams every day, so I am building
> my Wispr Flow portfolio entirely by voice, in Emergent.

That's 61 words. **Send me both times, and the words Flow got wrong.** I work out the speeds.

4. **Names.** Say each sentence on its own, **before** adding anything to Flow's dictionary:

> I built Skipp for SRM students.

> Drishti runs on Sarvam.

> Recoup works with Razorpay.

> I'm building it in Emergent with Wispr Flow.

> SRMIST is in Kattankulathur.

> Allotiq books rooms on campus.

> Tanglish is how I text my friends.

5. **Fix and retest.** For every name Flow got wrong, add it to Flow's dictionary, then say that
   sentence again. **Send me** which ones were wrong, and whether the dictionary fixed them. That's
   your "where voice broke, and the fix" story.

## E-1 · Voice diary (every evening, 1 minute)

Say one line into a note, then screenshot the Flow Hub as `assets/flow-hub/DATE.png`:

> Today I used Flow for [what you used it for]. It [worked / failed] when [where, or why].

**For example:** "Today I used Flow for my WhatsApp messages and my Emergent prompts. It failed in the
library, because I couldn't talk there." Only say what actually happened that day.

## E-3 · Android setup audit (Sunday, 15 minutes)

1. **Phone ready:** Do Not Disturb on, so no notifications get recorded. Open a WhatsApp chat with
   yourself.
2. **Uninstall Flow.** Your account and dictionary come back when you sign in again.
3. **Start Android's screen recorder:** Quick Settings → Screen record.
4. **Play Store** → search "Wispr Flow" → install → open. Sign in with **your challenge email**, and
   allow and set up whatever it asks.
5. In the WhatsApp chat, dictate:

> This is my first message with Flow on this phone.

6. Stop recording as soon as that sentence appears correctly.
7. **Compare with Gboard.** Start a new recording. In the same chat, tap Gboard's mic and say the same
   sentence. Stop.
8. **Send me** both recordings' file names, your phone model and your Android version. I count the
   steps, the permission screens and the time.

*Optional:* the same on a family member's phone, with their OK. They tap, and you only answer their
questions. In the repo they're "family member, about 50": no names.

## E-4 · Review check (Monday, 15 minutes)

I read public reviews and posts about Wispr Flow from Indian users, and sort each one into a theme. On
Monday I send you **20 of them, picked at random, without my themes**. For each, reply with its main
theme:

| Theme | It's mainly about |
|---|---|
| language | accent, Hindi, Hinglish, Tamil or other Indian languages, mixing languages |
| setup | installing, permissions, the keyboard, getting it to work in an app |
| accuracy | wrong words not caused by language (names, jargon, noise) |
| speed | slow, crashes, stops listening, needs internet |
| price | the price, free-plan limits, ₹ pricing, worth paying for |
| privacy | data, recordings, screen access, trust |
| praise | what it's good for (messages, email, notes, code) |
| other | anything else |

I compare your themes with mine. If we agree on 14 or more of the 20, the counts go on the site.
If not, they go on as "unchecked", or come off.
